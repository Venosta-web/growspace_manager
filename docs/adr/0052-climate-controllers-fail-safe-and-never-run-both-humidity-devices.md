# Climate Controllers Fail Safe, and Never Run Both Humidity Devices

**Status:** Accepted

A humidifier and dehumidifier are switched by VPD, and their controller did nothing at all when it lost that VPD. An unavailable sensor made the check return early. A frozen one sent no events, and events were the only thing that ran the check. Either way the device stayed in its last state indefinitely, and a humidifier left on in a flowering room with a dead sensor can soak it. With overlapping thresholds the humidifier and dehumidifier could also run together, each fighting the other. The exhaust, having lost every regulation sensor, held its last speed. Every command was sent with `blocking=False`, so a device that refused one was never seen (#792). ADR-0051 gave the climate controllers availability and plausibility, and left freshness and a fail-safe to this decision.

**Freshness uses the moisture sensor's rule, and each quantity has its own cap.** The humidifier, dehumidifier and exhaust read their control inputs through a `SensorWatch`, the same learned [[Observation Validity Window]] ADR-0051 uses. The cap is `climate_fail_safe_config.sensor_stale_after_minutes` (default 30, `0` off), not the irrigation setting of the same name. The question "how long may I act on this reading?" has a different answer for a tent's air than for its substrate, and a grower who turns one off should not turn the other off with it. All the controllers of a growspace share one `ClimateSafety`, and so one watch per sensor: a VPD sensor that the humidifier and dehumidifier both read learns one cadence and has one invalid episode.

**Hold through a dropout, fail safe after the timeout.** An invalid reading holds the last command, which is what these controllers have always done, and is right for a short dropout. When every input of a controller has been invalid for `sensor_timeout_minutes` (default 10, the delay HAGR's sensor-unavailability watchdog uses), the controller goes to its **Safe State**. For the humidifier and dehumidifier that is `off` by default, `on` or `hold` by choice. They may not both be `on`, which would break the interlock. The exhaust runs at `exhaust_fallback_speed` (default 50 %, clamped into its own speed range) once _every_ regulation sensor has failed; while any one reads, the combined demand simply leaves the dead terms out. Circulation has no Safe State: moving the air inside the tent does no harm whatever the room is doing, so it holds. A Safe State is applied past the short-cycle timers, since a minimum runtime exists to protect a compressor, not to keep a humidifier on for an hour after its sensor died. Each controller also re-evaluates once a minute, as well as on sensor events, so a frozen sensor is noticed at all.

**One episode, one alert.** A dead VPD sensor takes the humidifier and dehumidifier down in the same minute. Two pushes about one sensor would teach a grower to ignore both. The **Fail-Safe Episode** therefore belongs to the growspace, not to a controller. Every evaluation looks at all the growspace's controllers at once, so the first to find the sensor dead also finds the other. The episode raises one push on the `SENSOR_INVALID` tier and one persistent notification, which names every controller in its Safe State and what each is doing. A controller that joins later only rewrites the persistent notification. The episode ends with one recovery push when the last controller can see again, and a flapping sensor alerts at most once an hour, the ADR-0051 limit.

**The later demand wins the interlock.** When the humidifier and dehumidifier both want to run, one of them has to lose. Letting the humidifier always win would favour mould. Letting the dehumidifier always win would favour stress, and either fixed rule ignores what the room just did. "Later demand" is the one rule that responds to the room: the device whose VPD threshold was crossed most recently switches its partner off, and the partner waits for it to finish. Waiting, rather than taking over again on its next tick, is what stops two overlapping bands from ping-ponging every minute. A partner running with no demand of its own — switched on by hand, or held by its Safe State — always gives way, and so does a controller in its Safe State, which has no demand. Each takeover writes a logbook note on the device that was switched off.

**A runtime cap is a break, not a fault.** `humidifier_max_runtime_minutes` and `dehumidifier_max_runtime_minutes` (default 0, off) switch a device off after it has been seen on that long without a break. They apply whether or not VPD can be read at the time. The device's minimum off time then gives it its rest, and demand brings it back. Nothing latches: a humidifier that has run for three hours is a reason to look, not a reason to stop controlling. A fault latch for climate devices is left out of this decision.

**Commands are awaited, bounded, and counted.** Every actuator command now blocks, bounded by 10 s. A refusal or a timeout is logged and returned as `False`, never raised, so one broken plug cannot stop a controller commanding the others. The controllers count it as `climate.command_failure.<role>` in the reliability evidence (#796), beside `climate.fail_safe.<role>`, `climate.interlock` and `climate.max_runtime_stop`. The bound is short enough that a 10 s tick is not held for long by a hung cloud port. Grow lights share the drivers, and so share the bounded wait, but are not counted: they are not a climate control.

All of it is configured on one new sub-config, `EnvironmentConfig.climate_fail_safe_config`, rather than on `humidifier_config` or `exhaust_fan_config`. No card tab that replaces a device's config whole can drop it, for the same reason the Light Leak Guard (#794) has a sub-config of its own.
