# A Person at the Equipment Is Noticed, and Respected

**Status:** Accepted

Growspace Manager neither noticed nor respected a person operating its equipment. Nothing watched a managed pump between cycles, so a pump switched on by hand, or by a glitching relay, went unseen. A scheduled cycle that found it already running "confirmed" it at once — `_async_wait_for_switch_state` returned True on the first read — booked the person's water as its own, and switched the pump off under them at the end. The exhaust and circulation fans reasserted their computed speed every tick, fighting anyone who changed them (#793). HA-Irrigation-Strategy answers the same problem with a timed per-zone manual override that survives a restart, and a reconcile that treats any switch changed since a shot opened it as a person's.

**Two ways a person takes over, and they are different things.** A **Manual Override** is _declared_: `set_override` names a growspace, a subsystem and a duration. An **Unexpected On** is _observed_: a managed pump reads ON with no cycle of ours in flight. They share the controller's reason list and the Safety Ledger, but not a mechanism, because one is a promise made in advance and the other is a fact noticed after it.

**An Unexpected On is a person's by default.** The `alert` policy notifies once, holds every cycle — manual runs through Growspace Manager included, since the pump is already running — with `override_detected`, and leaves the pump alone until it reads OFF. The alternative, switching off anything we did not start, would turn off a grower hand-watering a dry pot, which is the commonest reason a pump is on. It is also the one reading of the event that cannot make a safe situation worse: a person watching a pump they started does not need it stopped, and a relay that glitched ON is reported at once. `enforce_off` is the opt-in for the grower who would rather have a stuck relay stopped than a hand-watering interrupted. It switches off, reads back and latches a Fault — `fault_unexpected_on:<entity>`, or `fault_off_unconfirmed:<entity>` with its retries when the pump will not read OFF, so a restart resumes stopping it. The Fault is deliberate: a pump that turned itself on is a hardware question an administrator should answer before irrigation resumes.

**In flight means commanded and not yet read back OFF.** The in-flight record is the set of outputs a cycle has sent `turn_on` to, cleared once OFF reads back, together with outputs under OFF retries or being switched off by `enforce_off` right now. It is set _before_ `turn_on`, because the state event can arrive while the command is still being awaited. The pump is also read before a cycle is admitted, so a pump that went on while nothing was watching — before a start, or on a fixture with no watch — can still never be confirmed as ours.

**A Manual Override means no command at all.** For its duration Growspace Manager sends the subsystem nothing: no cycle, no regulation tick, no Climate Fail-Safe, no light-leak OFF, no `enforce_off`. Anything less is still fighting the person, just less often. Every controller already asked `automation_enabled` before each command (#791), so the override is the same question narrowed to a subsystem: `IrrigationSafetyStore.commands_allowed(growspace, subsystem)`. The emergency stop is the one exception. It is not a controller's command but an operator's, and it must reach every output whatever anyone was told. An irrigation override that starts while a cycle of ours runs closes that cycle first, so the cycle's own OFF is the last command the pump gets and the person takes over a stopped pump, not a running one. A cycle stopped before its `turn_on` now sends no OFF either: Growspace Manager stops only what it started.

**Every override ends by itself.** A duration is required and at most 24 hours. An override nobody remembers setting is how a tent goes unregulated for a week, and a day covers any maintenance a person does by hand. It is written through to the safety store with the caller's HA user and reason before the call returns, survives a restart, expires on its own timer, and one that ran out while Home Assistant was stopped expires at the next start with a ledger row like any other. A stored override that cannot be read fails closed with the rest of the safety record. That is the one direction a lost override can safely fall: dropping it would silently hand control back mid-maintenance. When an irrigation override ends, a pump the person left running is an Unexpected On like any other. Any user may set one: like the automation switch it only ever takes commands away.

**Where it shows.** An irrigation override and a detected one read `inhibited` on the Irrigation Controller with `manual_override` and `override_detected`, on a configured controller even when nothing is automated, because they hold manual runs too. The sensor's new `overrides` attribute lists every Manual Override of the growspace, so the fans' overrides are visible even though the sensor is irrigation's. Reliability Evidence counts `irrigation.readback.unexpected_on` wherever it is seen, no longer only at a start, and the two new skip reasons.

Out of scope: a pump a person switches OFF _during_ one of our cycles. HAIS treats that as the person's too, and the readback already treats it as a cycle that ended early. Deciding how much water it delivered is #549's.
